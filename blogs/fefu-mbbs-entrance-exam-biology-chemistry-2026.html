FEFU MBBS Entrance Exam 2026: Biology and Chemistry Guide

MBBS Abroads Editorial Team September 24, 2026 7 min read
Before you prepare: Entrance subjects, assessment format and dates are intake-specific. This guide summarizes official FEFU materials available for review; the current admission notice for your cohort takes priority.

Applicants searching for the FEFU MBBS entrance exam usually want one useful answer: what should I study first? For the English-track General Medicine program, recent FEFU admission materials list Biology and Chemistry. The exact syllabus, format and passing scores should still be checked in the rules for your intake.

Quick answer: Start with the current FEFU syllabus, not a coaching-centre guess. Keep the FEFU university exam separate from NEET: they are different assessments with different purposes.

How to Prepare Without Wasting Study Time

  1. Download the latest subject requirements from FEFU’s applicant portal and save the dated copy.
  2. Mark each Biology and Chemistry topic as strong, review-needed or unfamiliar.
  3. Practise explaining concepts aloud; medical study rewards understanding, not only memorized definitions.
  4. Use timed question sets after you have reviewed the concepts and keep an error log.
  5. Ask the university whether the assessment includes a language check or any additional step.

Do not assume a NEET score replaces FEFU’s own entrance process. Likewise, a FEFU entrance result does not answer the separate Indian NEET eligibility question. Treat the university’s offer and the Indian regulatory requirements as two parallel checks.

If you are still gathering documents, follow the step-by-step FEFU admission guide and the Indian student document checklist.

Questions to Send the Admissions Office

Ask for the current exam subjects, syllabus, exam language, duration, scoring scale, minimum result, booking process, available dates and any rules for retakes. Request answers from an official FEFU channel and keep the message with your application records. If a representative gives a different answer, ask them to point to the university notice.
