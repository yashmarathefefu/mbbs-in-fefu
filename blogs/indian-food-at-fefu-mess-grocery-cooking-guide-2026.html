Food is rarely the reason a student chooses a medical university, but it can become the reason the first semester feels harder than expected. Regular meals affect concentration, sleep, immunity and budget.

Realistic answer: do not plan around one permanent “Indian mess” promise. Operators and menus can change. A more dependable FEFU plan combines campus food, grocery shopping and simple shared-kitchen cooking, with current arrangements confirmed before arrival.

A First-Month Food Plan That Actually Works

Week 1: reduce decisions

Carry a small customs-compliant starter supply of familiar dry snacks, ready mixes and spices. Find drinking water, breakfast, a grocery route and the current kitchen rules before experimenting.

Weeks 2–3: build five basic meals

Rice, dal, eggs or another protein, vegetables, oats and yogurt can form a practical base. For a strict vegetarian diet, identify reliable protein and read labels carefully.

Week 4: set a food budget

Track what you actually spend. A cafeteria meal, groceries and occasional delivery belong in different categories. A budget based on another student’s old screenshot is not your budget.

Questions to Ask About Your Assigned Hostel

  • Is a common kitchen available in this building and on which floor?
  • Which appliances are permitted or prohibited?
  • Is refrigerator/storage space shared?
  • What are cleaning and fire-safety rules?
  • Which current campus shops or cafes operate near the building?

Do not buy a guaranteed-mess claim. Ask for the current operator, sample menu, price, service period and cancellation terms. Food availability changes faster than university infrastructure.
