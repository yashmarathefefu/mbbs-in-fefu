“English medium” can mean several things on an admissions page. It may refer to lectures, while exams, ward conversations or some practical instructions work differently. A serious applicant should turn one marketing phrase into five written questions.

Direct answer: apply only after you receive current written confirmation for your exact FEFU General Medicine cohort covering lecture language, examination language and clinical teaching. At the same time, expect to learn Russian for patient communication and daily life. Those two facts can coexist.

Why Russian Is an Advantage, Not a Contradiction

A patient does not become an English speaker because a student enrolled in an English-taught program. History-taking depends on small details—where pain started, what made it worse, which medicines were taken and what the family observed. A translator cannot replace every bedside conversation.

Students who start Russian early usually have a better chance of following ward routines and speaking with local patients. The goal is not literary fluency. It is safe, respectful medical communication.

A sensible language plan

  • Year 1: daily vocabulary, campus navigation and pronunciation.
  • Year 2: anatomy terms, symptoms, numbers and instructions.
  • Year 3 onward: structured history-taking, consent phrases and ward communication.

The NMC/FMGL Question Students Miss

NMC rules are the reason Indian students should keep evidence of program duration, internship, medium and eligibility. Avoid the shortcut “NMC approved university.” NMC does not replace your responsibility to verify the foreign program against current rules. Keep the written medium confirmation in your permanent admission file.

Red flag: a representative says “everything is English” but refuses to state whether that includes exams and clinical teaching for your specific batch.
