Parents usually ask this as a yes-or-no question. The honest answer has conditions. A university name by itself does not complete the Indian-registration process; the student’s exact program, eligibility, internship, documents and future licensing performance all matter.

Short answer: FEFU can be evaluated as a foreign medical education option, but an Indian student must verify the exact General Medicine program against current NMC/FMGL rules. A directory listing or the words “NMC approved” should never replace that check.

WDOMS Listing Is Useful—but Commonly Misrepresented

The World Directory of Medical Schools is a directory, not a promise that every graduate can practise in every country. Use it to confirm the institution and school details. Then move to the rules of the country where you want registration.

For India, that means reading current NMC/FMGL requirements, not relying on a screenshot titled “WHO/NMC approved.” WHO does not issue a personal licence to practise medicine in India.

Questions to Put to FEFU Before Admission

  1. What is the official program title, duration and instruction language for my cohort?
  2. Where and how are clinical rotations completed?
  3. What internship or practical-training period is part of the qualification?
  4. Does the qualification make graduates eligible for the local licensing route, subject to local requirements?
  5. Which transcripts, logbooks and internship records will the graduate receive?

Avoid guarantees: no university or agent can promise a licensing-exam result six years in advance. A credible plan protects eligibility and documentation, then expects the student to earn the result.
